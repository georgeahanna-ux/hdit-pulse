HD IT Pulse · user guide · v0.6.5

Using Pulse

Pulse is where HD IT runs EOS: the weekly Scorecard, Rocks, Issues, To-dos and the Tuesday L10, with Pipeline, Marketing ROI and Whitespace Matrix alongside. One Microsoft 365 sign-in for all of it.

Getting in

Signing in

Sign in with your HD IT Microsoft 365 account. Microsoft asks for multi-factor authentication, and Pulse only opens on an HD IT managed device. If Microsoft says your device isn't compliant, contact the service desk.

What you see depends on your Pulse groups in Entra ID. If something is missing, open Your access from your account menu (top right). It lists your roles, what each one gives you and which group it comes from. Group changes apply the next time you sign in.

Finding your way

Home, Company, Divisions and seats

  • Home is yours: your seat on the Accountability Chart, your To-dos, your Rocks this quarter, the measurables you own and your apps. The leadership team also sees progress to the FY27 plan at the top.
  • Company holds what the whole business runs on: the Scorecard, Rocks, the V/TO and the Accountability Chart. The leadership team also has the Issues List, company To-dos and the FY plan.
  • Divisions lists your division (Leadership sees all six). A division page shows its key result, its apps, its numbers on the company Scorecard and its seats. Use the tabs for the division's own Scorecard, Rocks, Issues and To-dos.
  • Seats: while you're in a division, the menu lists its seats. Each seat page shows its roles, key result, who sits in it, the measurables it owns, its Rocks and its apps. "Proposed role scorecard" lists the few numbers the seat could track; they switch on once agreed with the seat's owner.
The weekly rhythm

The Scorecard

A Scorecard week runs Monday to Sunday, Sydney time. Tuesday's L10 at 9:30am reviews the week that has just ended. The grid shows the last 13 weeks, newest on the right; scroll left for earlier weeks. Select a measurable's name to see what it measures and how it's worked out.

Green cells are on target; cells outlined in red are off target. A lilac dot means the value was overridden.

Entering and overriding values

  • Select a cell to change it. Leadership can change any measurable. Everyone else can change their own division's measurables.
  • While a week is open (until 5pm on the Tuesday after it ends), type the value straight in.
  • After a week locks, or for a measurable that fills in automatically, a change is an override. Every override needs a reason. The original value stays visible beside it.
  • An override applies to that week only. You can remove it later, with a reason.
  • Every entry, override and removal is recorded in the audit log: who, when, old value, new value and reason.

Automatic values, on trial first

Pulse works out some measurables itself every hour. Each one starts on trial: the value you type in is still the one that counts, and the automatic value is shown under it in smaller type so you can compare the two. Untick Show automatic values on trial to hide them.

  • Select a cell to see how the automatic value was worked out, for example "142 of 150 tickets had a first response in time".
  • Select the measurable's name to see the typed-in and automatic values side by side for recent weeks.
  • When they agree, Leadership switches the measurable to automatic from the same panel, with a reason. From then on open weeks fill in by themselves; locked weeks can still be overridden. Leadership can switch it back the same way.
  • Leadership can select Work out now to refresh the automatic values within a few minutes.

From 0.6.5, also on trial: MQLs from Marketing ROI (leads that first reached MQL or beyond that week; leads already at MQL before Marketing ROI 0.7.0 are dated by when the opportunity was created, and the week says how many); New MRR signed (the monthly revenue on opportunities won that week, Stage 9: Closed Won) and Weighted pipeline (Stage 6 and 7 one-off revenue × probability, as Pipeline shows it) from Autotask; and CSAT (share of the week's ratings that were positive, as SimpleSAT judges them) and NPS (over the 90 days to the end of the week) from SimpleSAT. Autotask keeps no history of an opportunity's stage, so weighted pipeline is a reading taken every hour during the week: the last one before the week ends stands, and weeks before 0.6.5 stay as typed in.

From Xero (0.6.4), also on trial: cash on hand, debtor days, services gross margin and EBITDA %; the monthly ones show the month so far and are provisional until Finance closes the month. On trial: SLA first response, tickets closed per tech per day, first-contact resolution and billable utilisation (from 0.5.3), and seats serviced and new seats signed (from 0.5.4), all from Autotask. A tech is anyone whose default department in Autotask is Service Desk. A seat is one unit of IT Support & Proactive Maintenance - Business Class Plan, Technical Services and Management, or IT Managed Services Support - Business Class - Per User per Month. New seats are the seats on opportunities won that week.

EOS

Rocks, Issues, To-dos and the V/TO

  • Rocks are this quarter's priorities. Leadership sets company Rocks; a division adds its own; anyone can add an individual Rock for themselves. Set each Rock on track, off track, done or not done, and tick off its milestones.
  • Issues: the company Issues List belongs to the leadership team; each division has its own on its page. Number your top three for IDS, then solve (with the solution) or drop each one.
  • To-dos are seven-day commitments, due a week from when they're added unless you choose a date. Tick them off when done. From 0.8.0 they also appear in Planner.
  • V/TO: everyone can read it; Leadership edits it. Every version is kept.
FY27

The FY plan and progress bars

The leadership team and Finance see progress to the V/TO 1-year plan for FY27 (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027).

  • Revenue and New seats add up month by month. Each quarter's bar shows that quarter's total against its target.
  • MRR and Seats are month-end levels. The year bar shows how far we've come from where the year started (30 June) to the target. Each quarter's bar shows the level at the end of that quarter against its target.
  • The yellow tick shows how much of the year, or the quarter, has gone. "On pace" means the bar is at or past the tick.
  • Leadership sets the year targets and the opening levels. Leadership and Finance set quarter targets and enter month-end figures under Company → FY plan. Changing a figure that's already there needs a reason.

From 0.6.0, revenue comes from Xero and MRR, seats and new seats from Autotask automatically.

MRR, Seats and New seats also fill in from Autotask, on trial first: the automatic figure shows under each month. Leadership or Finance select Switch to automatic once it matches. A figure Finance enters always takes priority. MRR is recurring contract services converted to a monthly amount.

Apps

Pipeline, Marketing ROI and Whitespace Matrix

The apps sit in the menu under the division they serve: Pipeline, Marketing ROI and Whitespace Matrix are under Sales & Marketing. If you can open an app but don't see its division, it's under Your apps.

Each app opens inside Pulse and signs you in with your Microsoft 365 account, so there's no separate password. Your role in each app (user, editor or administrator) comes from its Pulse group. Open on its own opens the app's own address in a new tab.

Platform Admins

Connections

Under Platform → Connections, Platform Admins enter the credentials Pulse uses to read Autotask, SimpleSAT, Xero, Datto RMM and IT Glue. Each card says what the connection is for and the permissions its API user needs.

Autotask uses one API user for all of Pulse. Pulse itself limits what each part can do with it: the Scorecard only reads, People and departments can only change resource departments, and Projects can only edit tasks and add time entries. Nothing in Pulse can delete Autotask records.

  • Select Enter, type the details and save. Autotask details are tested first; Pulse only saves them if Autotask accepts them.
  • Pulse never shows a credential again. To change one, select Replace and give a reason.
  • Set an owner and a rotation-due date for each connection. Pulse flags it when rotation is overdue.
  • Every change is recorded in the audit log with your name and reason. The credential itself is never recorded.
  • Xero: enter the Xero app's client id and secret on the Xero app card (its redirect URI is shown there), then select Connect to Xero on the Xero connection card. Sign in to Xero, choose HD IT's organisation and allow access. Pulse can only read reports and the chart of accounts. If the connection lapses, select Reconnect.
Managed Services

Projects

Under Managed Services → Projects (Leadership, Project Managers and Managed Services) every open Autotask project is on one Gantt chart, grouped by client. Project templates aren't shown.

  • Bar colours show health: on track (aqua), at risk (lemon: a task is overdue, or progress is more than 15 points behind the time gone), late (red: past its end date), not started (lilac) and done (grey). The darker part of each bar is how much is done, by hours.
  • Use the buttons to show only late or at-risk projects, zoom by weeks, months or quarters, and jump to today. Select ▸ beside a project to see its phases.
  • Select a project's name or bar to open its own Gantt chart: phases, tasks, milestones (diamonds) and the arrows between dependent tasks. Tick Critical path to outline the tasks that set the finish date, and Baseline to see where each task was planned when the baseline was saved.
  • Select a task for its dates, hours, how many days it could slip (float) and what it's waiting for.
  • Logging time: on a task you're assigned to, select Log time, enter the day, hours and what you did, and tick the box if it finishes the task. It's saved to Autotask as your time entry. Mark complete closes the task in Autotask. Tick My tasks only to see just yours. (Your Pulse account must be linked to your Autotask resource; see People and departments.)
  • Rescheduling (Project Managers and Leadership): drag a bar to move a task, or drag its left or right end to change its start or end. Dates land on working days. When an end date moves, the tasks that depend on it move by the same number of working days; when a start date moves, the tasks before it move. Pulse lists every task that will change, with old and new dates. Nothing changes in Autotask until you select Save to Autotask. Undo appears straight after.
  • Linking tasks (Project Managers and Leadership): select Link tasks, then tick tasks in the order they should happen (a number shows the order). Select Link and choose: one after another; all after the first one ticked; or the last one ticked after all the others. Add a gap in working days if needed. Leave Move tasks that would start too early ticked and Pulse moves any task that would start before what it waits for. You see every new dependency and date change before Save to Autotask. To remove a dependency, select the task and choose Remove beside it.
  • Save baseline keeps today's dates; the thin grey bars under each task then show where it was planned.

Resource planner

Projects → Resource planner shows each person's project hours allocated against what they have available, week by week. Each open task's remaining hours are spread evenly over its remaining working days; overdue work counts in this week. Available hours are 7.6 a working day, less public holidays and leave booked in Autotask, less a share kept for service desk and other work.

  • Colours: lilac under 60%, light aqua 60–85%, aqua 85–100%, red over-allocated. A striped line means leave that week; a red dot means overdue work is included.
  • Select a person to see the tasks behind each week. Percent shows utilisation instead of hours.
  • Project Managers set the share kept for other work, and which departments always appear, under Capacity settings.

Project lead time

For Sales and Project Managers (Sales & Marketing → Project lead time): how much project work is queued, how many hours a week the project team gets through, and the week a new project could realistically start. Enter the new project's size and how many engineers it needs for an estimated finish. Under What if, add people (how many, their project hours a week, and from when) to see how much sooner work could start. What-ifs aren't saved anywhere.

  • Pulse reads Autotask every 15 minutes. Refresh from Autotask re-reads the project now.
Leadership and Platform Admins

People and departments

Under Platform → People and departments:

  • Directory lists everyone in Pulse and the Autotask account they're matched to. Pulse matches by email address. If a match is wrong, a Platform Admin selects Change link, picks the right account (or "No Autotask account") and gives a reason.
  • Autotask departments shows who has each default department. A tech is anyone whose default department is Service Desk; they're who the Scorecard counts for tickets per tech and utilisation.
  • To move people, tick them, select Change default department, choose the department, give a reason and select Change in Autotask. Pulse changes Autotask straight away. If someone isn't in that department yet, Pulse adds them with their current role unless you choose one.
  • Needs review (Platform Admins) lists people Pulse couldn't match on its own. Select Fix to link them.
  • Pulse reads Autotask every 15 minutes and Microsoft 365 once a day. Refresh reads them now.
Record keeping

The audit log

Pulse records every sign-in, change, override and view of a sensitive page (finance detail, People data, the audit log and Connections). The record is kept in the Pulse database, where it can't be edited or deleted, and in Azure for 12 months. Platform Admins and Compliance can search it under Platform → Audit log.
